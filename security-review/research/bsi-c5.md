# BSI IT-Grundschutz and C5: what German public-sector customers would measure open-dpp against

Research ticket [#777](https://github.com/open-dpp/open-dpp/issues/777) of map [#772](https://github.com/open-dpp/open-dpp/issues/772). Slug `bsi-c5`. Researched 2026-09-03 against primary sources on bsi.bund.de, gesetze-im-internet.de and the official OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 text. Vocabulary (Control, Control Register, Ring, Instance Profile) follows `CONTEXT.md` § Security Review. BSI identifiers and quoted requirement titles stay German.

## Question

If a German public-sector or regulated customer asks for "BSI alignment": which IT-Grundschutz-Kompendium building blocks (Bausteine) apply to open-dpp as a containerised NestJS/Vue application with MongoDB and S3-compatible object storage, operated as SaaS on a single VM per instance? Which C5 criteria would the SaaS operator be measured against, and how does a C5 attestation work? Which of those requirements are not already covered by OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 and therefore need to become additional Controls?

## Short answer

1. **Edition 2023 of the IT-Grundschutz-Kompendium is the current and only valid edition in 2026.** BSI published no 2024, 2025 or 2026 edition, corrects it via binding Errata (latest Stand 26.03.2026), names it as the audit basis in the Prüfgrundlage of 01.02.2026, and states that it "bleibt in der mehrjährigen Übergangszeit anwendbar" while the machine-readable successor **Grundschutz++** is piloted (pilot 01.04.–30.09.2026, public release at it-sa 27.10.2026, certification from 01.01.2027). Plan against Edition 2023 now; expect to re-key identifiers to Grundschutz++ rules in 2027.
2. **Applicable Bausteine** (all Edition 2023): APP.3.1 Webanwendungen und Webservices, APP.3.2 Webserver, APP.4.3 Relationale Datenbanken (applied *sinngemäß* to MongoDB, since BSI has no NoSQL block and APP.4.3 excludes non-relational DBs), CON.8 Software-Entwicklung, CON.10 Entwicklung von Webanwendungen, OPS.1.1.2 Ordnungsgemäße IT-Administration, OPS.1.1.3 Patch- und Änderungsmanagement, OPS.1.1.4 Schutz vor Schadprogrammen, OPS.1.1.5 Protokollierung, OPS.1.1.6 Software-Tests und -Freigaben, SYS.1.6 Containerisierung (plus SYS.1.1/SYS.1.3 for the host), DER.2.1 Behandlung von Sicherheitsvorfällen, CON.3 Datensicherungskonzept, CON.1 Kryptokonzept, ORP.4 Identitäts- und Berechtigungsmanagement, and — the SaaS-provider block — **OPS.3.2 Anbieten von Outsourcing** (applied once per customer). APP.4.4 Kubernetes is *not* applicable to docker-compose.
3. **C5 is in transition.** C5:2020 (121 criteria) is superseded by **C5:2026** (final 07.04.2026, v1.1.0 of 11.08.2026; 17 domains, 168 criteria, EUCS-"Substantial"-aligned sub-criteria). C5:2026 becomes mandatory for Typ-1 reports dated on/after 1 June 2027 and Typ-2 periods starting on/after that date; earlier application is allowed. A C5 "attestation" is an ISAE 3000 (Revised) / IDW PS 860 reasonable-assurance report by an independent auditor (in Germany a Wirtschaftsprüfer) over the provider's system description — Typ 1 (design, first engagement only) or Typ 2 (effectiveness over a period); BSI issues nothing, checks nothing and keeps no list. Federal customers are bound by the BSI Mindeststandard zur Nutzung externer Cloud-Dienste v2.1 (NCD.2.1.02 b: C5 Basiskriterien MUSS; NCD.2.2.01 e: Typ-2 report as evidence; j) iii: audit right if no report). Health-sector cloud processing needs a C5 Typ-2 Testat by law (§ 393 SGB V) — relevant only if open-dpp ever hosts health data.
4. **Gap vs ASVS 5.0 L2.** ASVS 5.0 deliberately scopes itself to the product and excludes CI/CD, hosting, operations, backup, SIEM/alerting and all SDLC process requirements (moved to Appendix D). Everything BSI/C5 demand in those areas is therefore *not* covered by L2 and must become additional Controls in Rings 2–4: backup and restore testing, log retention/central logging/monitoring, OS/container patching, vulnerability-management SLAs with scans and pentests, incident response and customer notification, vulnerability disclosure, container/host hardening, administrator access governance, tenant-separation concept, SaaS security concept with contracts/exit/deletion, SDLC policy with release approval and environment separation, malware-signature currency, business continuity, supplier management and asset inventory. Section "Implications" lists 24 concrete Controls with BSI and C5 identifiers.

8. **Rule when no block fits (MongoDB, MinIO).** Kompendium ch. 2 "Schichtenmodell und Modellierung": "Bei der Modellierung … kann es Zielobjekte geben, die mit den vorliegenden Bausteinen nicht hinreichend abgebildet werden können. In diesem Fall muss eine Risikoanalyse durchgeführt werden" and "allgemeine Bausteine [stellen] eine gute Grundlage für die Modellierung und Risikoanalyse dar, wenn für ein konkretes Zielobjekt kein spezifischer Baustein existiert." BSI-Standard 200-2 ch. 8.3: "Generell sollten die Anforderungen der IT-Grundschutz-Bausteine immer sinngemäß umgesetzt werden. Alle Änderungen gegenüber dem IT-Grundschutz-Kompendium sollten dokumentiert werden". APP.4.3 ch. 1.3: "Sicherheitsanforderungen an nicht-relationale Datenbanksysteme sind nicht Gegenstand des vorliegenden Bausteins." No NoSQL or object-storage block exists among the 111 blocks. Consequence: model MongoDB with APP.4.3 *sinngemäß* plus a documented Risikoanalyse (BSI-Standard 200-3); model MinIO via SYS.1.6 (it is a container) plus CON.3 and the same Risikoanalyse pattern. Sources: Kompendium PDF (Finding 1); 200-2 PDF (Finding 7); APP.4.3 PDF (Finding 11).

### D. OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 — what it deliberately leaves out

37. **Release and levels.** ASVS 5.0.0, 30 May 2025 (no 5.0.x patch), 345 requirements in 17 chapters V1–V17; L2 = all L1 + L2 rows (~70 % of the standard). Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x03-What-is-the-ASVS.md ; https://api.github.com/repos/OWASP/ASVS/releases
38. **Explicit scope exclusions (0x03).** "ASVS does not prescribe development lifecycle activities or dictate how the application should be built via a CI/CD pipeline; instead, it specifies the security outcomes that must be achieved within the product itself." "ASVS requirements should be evaluated alongside guidance for other aspects of the application lifecycle, including CI/CD processes, hosting, and operational activities." "For instance, backing up the application or its data is usually the responsibility of an external process and is not controlled by the application or its developers." "All other considerations, such as functional aspects, code style, or policy requirements, are out of scope." V16.3: "while ASVS includes logging of security events in scope, alerting and correlation (e.g., SIEM rules or monitoring infrastructure) are considered out of scope". Source: 0x03 (Finding 37); https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x25-V16-Security-Logging-and-Error-Handling.md
39. **Process requirements were removed in 5.0.** 0x05: "The former V1 Architecture chapter has been removed. Its initial section contained requirements that were out of scope". Appendix D: "There are a number of security processes which were removed from ASVS 5.0 but are still a good idea. The OWASP SAMM project may be a good source" — secure SDLC, threat modelling per design change, security constraints in user stories, secure-coding checklist, backdoor/malicious-code review, configuration-drift detection, third-party configuration hardening; and non-mandatory in-scope items incl. `security.txt`. Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x05-For-Users-Of-4.0.md ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x93-Appendix-D_Recommendations.md

Notes for the register design:

- **Identifiers to carry.** Keep three columns — `ASVS 5.0`, `IT-GS 2023`, `C5:2026` — and plan a fourth for Grundschutz++ rule IDs once the Anwenderkatalog is public (October 2026 per BSI's Meilensteinplan). C5:2020 IDs are not worth carrying: new attestations after June 2027 must use C5:2026, and the BSI change history (English, published; German announced) maps old to new.
- **Modal-verb semantics.** For BSI-facing Controls the B/S distinction matters more than the ASVS level: a customer's auditor will treat Basis requirements as MUSS. Suggest tagging the twelve † rows as "mandatory for any BSI conversation".
- **Small-team fit.** C5:2026 Basis criteria assume a SIEM (OPS-13), monthly scans (OPS-25) and yearly pentests (OPS-22). A 3-person operator can meet the letter with the existing Ansible monitoring stack plus alert routing, CI-driven image/dependency scanning and the map's yearly external test; the *documentation* (policies per SP-01, system description) is the larger cost. C5 "korrespondierende Kundenkriterien" also let several duties be contractually shifted to whitelabel customers who host themselves.
- **Ring 4 becomes real.** Most of C-06, C-11, C-13, C-14, C-16, C-22 are policies rather than code; ASVS gives no template. Appendix D's pointer to OWASP SAMM is the reasonable process reference; BSI's Umsetzungshinweise for the blocks above (also on bsi.bund.de) contain concrete measure lists.

## Open questions

1. **Does any live customer actually require IT-Grundschutz or C5?** Federal bodies are bound by the Mindeststandard (see Finding 32); Länder, municipalities and regulated industry usually ask via questionnaires that mirror C5 domains. Until a concrete request exists, the recommended stance is "C5-ready documentation" (system description, policies, evidence) rather than commissioning an ISAE 3000 report — the report itself is a Wirtschaftsprüfer engagement outside this map's scope, like the TÜViT engagement.
2. **Grundschutz++ re-keying.** The Anwenderkatalog is expected 27.10.2026 with certification from 01.01.2027; whether BSI publishes an Edition-2023-to-GS++ mapping is not stated on the pages read. Revisit when the GitHub "Stand der Technik-Bibliothek" is public.
3. **MinIO modelling.** No BSI block fits S3-compatible object storage; SYS.1.6 (container) + CON.3 (backup) + OPS.1.1.5 (logging) plus a documented Risikoanalyse is the defensible approach. SYS.1.8 Speicherlösungen or APP.3.3 Fileserver were not evaluated and may fit better — check before the register is finalised.
4. **C5:2020 cross-walk.** C5:2020 was read only at title level (Finding 31); the Control table cites C5:2026 IDs. If a customer hands over a C5:2020-based questionnaire before mid-2027, the English change history at https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/CloudComputing/Anforderungskatalog/2026/C5_2026_Aenderungshistorie.html is the mapping source (German version and the C5:2026 cross-reference table to international standards "erfolgt zeitnah" per BSI).
5. **Self-hosted and whitelabel instances.** OPS.3.2 is applied once per customer and C5 shifts duties to customers via CUEC. The Deployment Security Guide (map deliverable) should state which of C-01…C-24 transfer to a self-hosting deployer.
6. **Umsetzungshinweise.** BSI publishes implementation hints per block that were not read here; they may sharpen C-09/C-10/C-20 into testable checks for the baseline run.
7. **Health data.** Digital Product Passports carry no Sozial-/Gesundheitsdaten, so § 393 SGB V and TR-03161 (Findings 33, 35) are recorded for completeness only. Should a customer ever attach health-related product data, both switch from "note" to "hard requirement" (C5 Typ-2 Testat, ISO 27001/IT-Grundschutz certificate for the backend operator).
