# ESPR / CEN-CENELEC JTC 24: security requirements binding a DPP system provider

- Ticket: [#775](https://github.com/open-dpp/open-dpp/issues/775) (research), part of map [#772](https://github.com/open-dpp/open-dpp/issues/772); feeds [#790](https://github.com/open-dpp/open-dpp/issues/790) (regulatory mapping).
- Researched: 2026-09-03, against primary sources only (EUR-Lex / Publications Office texts, Commission pages and documents, CEN-CENELEC announcements). Secondary write-ups were used only to locate primary documents.
- Status legend used below: **in force** (published in the OJ, applicable) · **applies from** (in force but obligation starts later) · **adopted, not yet cited** (standard ratified, no OJ reference yet) · **under preparation** (consultation stage, no draft text public) · **not found** (no trace as of research date).
- Vocabulary: Control, Control Register, Ring (1 software · 2 reference deployment · 3 operated instances · 4 organisation) as in map #772.

## Question

Which security-relevant requirements do the ESPR ((EU) 2024/1781, Art. 9–13 + secondary legislation) and the CEN/CENELEC JTC 24 DPP-system standards place on a DPP service provider — data persistence and backup (#596), the EU registry interface, access-rights management and authentication for restricted data, unique identifiers and data carriers, availability, integrity/authenticity of passport data, data-carrier resolution — each with its source and draft status, so the Control Register can adopt them as Controls?

## Short answer

1. The **binding layer today** is small and mostly in force: ESPR Art. 10–11 essential requirements (persistent UPI behind a data carrier, open/interoperable data, access regulated by access rights, back-up copy via an *independent* DPP service provider, availability beyond the operator's insolvency, restricted write rights, "data authentication, reliability and integrity", "high level of security and privacy … fraud is avoided", no re-use of hosted data by the provider), plus the **DPP registry** Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 (in force since 6 Aug 2026, registry live since 20 Jul 2026), which makes eIDAS-qualified identity verification, a resolvable HTTPS UPI URL, a hash of each DPP version and a verified back-up link concrete interface obligations for whoever registers passports.
2. The **technical layer** is now largely standardised: six harmonised EN standards (EN 18216, 18219, 18220, 18221, 18222, 18223) were cited in the OJ on 15 Jul 2026 (Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736) and carry presumption of conformity with Art. 10–11; the two *security* standards — **EN 18239** (access rights management, information system security, business confidentiality) and **EN 18246** (data authentication, reliability, integrity) — are ratified but **not yet cited** in the OJ (Commission plans a second decision in September 2026). The normative text of all eight is paywalled via national standards bodies; only scopes and titles are public.
3. The **service-provider layer is not yet law**: the delegated act under ESPR Art. 11 (third subparagraph) on requirements and a certification scheme for DPP service providers has passed call for evidence (Nov–Dec 2024) and public consultation (Apr–Jul 2025) but no draft act has been published; the Commission's own roadmap now puts adoption in Q2–Q3 2027. Its announced pillars — information security, availability/financial viability for long-term access, third-party assurance — are the strongest signal of what the register should anticipate.
4. The **Battery Regulation** (first concrete DPP, applies from 18 Feb 2027) mirrors the ESPR essential requirements almost verbatim (Art. 78) and adds a four-tier access model (Annex XIII: public · legitimate interest · authorities · per-item legitimate interest) that is the reference design for restricted-data authorisation.

Net: #596's "backup to fulfil CEN/CENELEC and EU registry requirements" is grounded in ESPR Art. 10(4) + Annex III(l) + Art. 11(e) and operationalised by Implementing Regulation 2026/1778 Art. 8(7)(e) and EN 18221 — but the back-up must be held by an **independent third party**, so an S3 copy inside the same open-dpp instance satisfies persistence, not the legal back-up requirement.

## Findings

### A. ESPR — Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 (primary law)

Source text: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj/eng> (ELI). Entered into force 18 July 2024 (Art. 80); the DPP obligations bite per product group once a delegated act under Art. 4 applies, and for batteries via Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (see E). Status of everything in this section: **in force**, applicability product-group-dependent.

1. **Definition of "DPP service provider" — Art. 2(32).** "a natural or legal person that is an independent third-party authorised by the economic operator which places the product on the market or puts it into service and that processes the digital product passport data for that product for the purpose of making such data available to economic operators and other relevant actors with a right to access those data under this Regulation or other Union law". → open-dpp operated as SaaS for a customer is a DPP service provider; a self-hosted open-dpp run by the economic operator itself is not (the operator then stores under Art. 11(c) first alternative).

2. **Accuracy and access design — Art. 9(1), 9(2)(f)–(i).** "The data in the digital product passport shall be accurate, complete and up to date." Delegated acts specify "the actors that are to have access to data … and to what data", "the actors that are to create … or update the data", "the detailed arrangements for introducing or updating data", and "the period during which the digital product passport is to remain available, which shall correspond to at least the expected lifetime of a specific product." → Access-control and retention policies are product-group parameters that the platform must be able to express, not constants.

3. **Essential requirements of the passport — Art. 10(1).** (a) "connected through a data carrier to a persistent unique product identifier"; (b) carrier "physically present on the product, its packaging or on documentation"; (c) carrier and UPI "shall comply with one or more of the standards referred to in Annex III, second paragraph, or equivalent … until the references of harmonised standards are published"; (d) all data "based on open standards, developed with an interoperable format, and shall be, as appropriate, machine-readable, structured, searchable, and transferable through an open interoperable data exchange network without vender lock-in"; (e) "personal data relating to customers shall not be stored in the digital product passport without their explicit consent"; (g) "the access to data … shall be regulated in accordance with the essential requirements set out in this Article and Article 11 and with the specific access rights at product group level".

4. **Mandatory back-up copy — Art. 10(4) and Annex III(l).** "The economic operator, when placing the product on the market, shall make available a back-up copy of the digital product passport through a digital product passport service provider." Annex III lists as passport data "(l) the reference of the digital product passport service provider hosting the back-up copy of the digital product passport." → The back-up is (i) a legal obligation of the economic operator, (ii) held by an *independent* third party (Art. 2(32)), (iii) referenced from inside the passport, and (iv) verified by the registry (finding 12). This is the primary source behind #596.

5. **Technical design and operation — Art. 11, first subparagraph.** (a) full technical/semantic/organisational interoperability with other DPPs; (b) listed actors "shall have free of charge and easy access … based on their respective access rights"; (c) "stored by the economic operator responsible for its creation or by digital product passport service providers"; (d) a new passport for a product that already has one "shall be linked to the original"; (e) "shall remain available for the period specified in delegated acts … including after an insolvency, a liquidation or a cessation of activity in the Union of the economic operator"; (f) "the rights to introduce, modify or update data … shall be restricted based on the access rights"; (g) "data authentication, reliability and integrity shall be ensured"; (h) "designed and operated so that a high level of security and privacy is ensured and fraud is avoided."

6. **Provider data-use restriction — Art. 11, second subparagraph.** Service providers "shall not sell, reuse or process such data, in whole or in part, beyond what is necessary for the provision of the relevant storing or processing services, unless specifically agreed with the economic operator". → A Ring-4 (organisational/contractual) Control plus a technical one: no analytics/LLM processing of passport content beyond the service without explicit customer agreement (relevant to the LangChain/Mistral feature).

7. **Empowerments for the provider layer — Art. 11, third and fourth subparagraphs.** Delegated acts "setting out the requirements that digital product passport service providers are to comply with in order to become such providers, and, where appropriate, a certification scheme", and implementing acts "setting out procedures to issue and verify the digital credentials of economic operators and other relevant actors that have access rights". Status of both: **under preparation / not found** (see D and F). Note: the empowerment is in Art. 11, not Art. 10(4)/(5) as sometimes cited.

8. **Identifiers — Art. 12 and Annex III second paragraph.** Operator and facility identifiers "shall comply with the standards referred to in Annex III … or equivalent … until the references of harmonised standards are published". Annex III: carrier, UPI, operator and facility identifiers "shall … comply with standards ISO/IEC 15459-1:2014, 15459-2:2015, 15459-3:2014, 15459-4:2014, 15459-5:2014 and 15459-6:2014"; GTIN "as provided for in … ISO/IEC 15459-6". Art. 12(4)–(5): delegated acts on "life cycle management of unique identifiers and of data carriers", incl. "rules to ensure that unique identifiers and data carriers are reliable, verifiable and unique globally" and "rules on creating, maintaining, updating, and withdrawing" them — **not found** as adopted or drafted (F).

9. **Registry and customs — Art. 13, 15.** "By 19 July 2026, the Commission shall set up a digital registry … which stores in a secure manner at least the unique identifiers"; for goods released for free circulation also the commodity code; Art. 13(2)(a) registry data may be extended per product group considering "the need to allow for the verification of the authenticity of the digital product passport"; Art. 13(4) "The economic operator … shall upload, in the registry, the data"; Art. 13(5) the registry "shall automatically communicate … a unique registration identifier" and the Commission "shall adopt an implementing act specifying the implementation arrangements" (→ finding 10). Art. 15: customs release only after the unique registration identifier and commodity code are verified against the registry. Art. 78 ESPR inserts Art. 77(10) into the Battery Regulation: the battery operator "shall upload the unique identifier in the registry".

10. **Presumption of conformity — Art. 41(2).** "Digital product passports which are in conformity with harmonised standards or parts thereof the references of which have been published in the Official Journal … shall be presumed to be in conformity with the requirements set out in Articles 10 and 11 to the extent that those requirements are covered". → Conformity with the EN 182xx series (C) is the cheapest evidence path for Art. 10–11.

### B. DPP registry — Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778

Source: <http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1778/oj> (adopted 16 Jul 2026, OJ L 17 Jul 2026, in force 20 days after publication = 6 Aug 2026; legal basis ESPR Art. 13(5)). Registry live at <https://registry.product-passport.ec.europa.eu> since 20 Jul 2026 per the Commission DPP page; test environment <https://registry.acc.product-passport.ec.europa.eu>. Status: **in force**.

11. **Registry architecture and provider list — Art. 3.** The registry consists of "(a) a website providing a secure user interface …; (b) an API for registering the digital product passport and receiving information from the registry; (c) a verification platform to confirm and verify the existence and completeness of the digital product passports; (d) a scheme for generating unique registration identifiers; (e) a storage component …; (f) a list of verified digital product passport service providers registered in the registry; (g) a semantic repository; (h) a log system; (i) identification and authorisation schemes". Recital 3: "the digital product passport system is built on a decentralised model". → A DPP service provider is itself an object of the registry (verified-provider list) and a potential API client.

12. **Automatic verification at registration — Art. 8(7)–(9).** On submission the Commission "shall read the digital product passport content and automatically confirm: (a) the semantic conformity …; (b) … coherence of the mandatory data …; (c) the conformity … with the granularity level …; (d) … the validity of the commodity code …; (e) where relevant, the link to the back-up hosted by a digital product passport service provider." The registry then stores "a unique and persistent registration identifier", "where relevant, reference to the digital product passport service provider" and "registrant information, including date and time of the registration and the integrity of the digital product passport as part of the evidence of the registration event." Art. 2(16) defines "hash of the version of the digital product passport" as "the output generated from the relevant electronic data using a cryptographic algorithm from the relevant version". → The passport must be machine-readable at its UPI URL at registration time, the back-up link must resolve, and a per-version cryptographic hash is part of the legal record.

13. **Identity verification via eIDAS — Art. 4, 5, 8(2), 19(4).** Legal persons become "verified economic operators" by "a qualified electronic seal supported by a qualified certificate for electronic seal, issued by a qualified trust service provider pursuant to Regulation (EU) No 910/2014" (or a qualified electronic attestation of attributes); natural persons via qualified electronic signature or eID at assurance level "high". Verification lapses when the means expire "but no longer than three years". Third parties acting for the operator "need to follow the verification process in accordance with Article 5" and "the verified economic operator shall remain fully responsible". The August 2026 User Guide adds the mechanics: EU Login authentication, an EC-sealed PDF declaration countersigned with QES/QSeal from a QSCD (software certificates are rejected), organisation identifier matched against the certificate's `organizationIdentifier` (OID 2.5.4.97). → If open-dpp registers passports on behalf of customers, the operating organisation needs its own qualified seal, a QSCD-backed key-management process, and a 3-year re-verification calendar.

14. **Security duties of the registrant — Art. 19(2)–(3), 20(2), 17.** The verified economic operator "shall ensure that the information stored in the registry … is kept accurate, complete and up to date at all times" and "shall be responsible for implementing appropriate technical and organisational security measures for its IT systems and credentials used to access the registry, to prevent unauthorised access to or modification of registration data through its IT system." Art. 17: any user "who becomes aware of, or has reasonable grounds to suspect, malicious behaviour in or against the registry shall immediately inform the Commission". Recital 31: the operator "should remain liable even if a third party is authorised to register … on behalf of the economic operator." → Credential storage for registry access (EU Login / API tokens, Art. 2(2)–(3)) and an incident-notification duty toward the Commission become Controls.

15. **Proof of registration — Art. 9.** A downloadable "secure electronic document" containing the UPI, commodity code, verified operator identity, the Commission-time-stamped registration date and "a hash of the version of the digital product passport", "guaranteed by means of a qualified electronic seal", available 90 days. → The platform must be able to reproduce the exact byte-level serialisation of a registered version to let anyone recompute the hash.

16. **Versioning, logging, retention — Art. 10, 14.** Every "creation, modification and deletion" of registration data is logged; the registry "shall support the versioning of the registered data, and store a timestamp of the Commission for each update"; default retention "10 years after registration" unless Union law sets a passport-availability period; registry logs keep access/authentication events 6 months, admin and data-exchange logs 5 years, data-modification logs for the life of the registration, with "immutability and confidentiality of the logs". → Not binding on the provider, but the Commission's own baseline for what "secure" means; a parity Control (version history + immutable audit log + comparable retention) is defensible.

17. **UPI URL constraints — Registry User Guide (Aug 2026, official, non-binding).** Source: <https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/079a45e2-469f-4eec-b1e5-32e8e05d1357_en?filename=dpp_registry_user_guide_for_economic_operators.pdf>. The UPI "needs to resolve to where you have hosted the DPP Data"; it "is a mandatory value conforming to a URL format compliant with JTC 24 standards. Max length is 2000 chars"; the passport data "Must be resolvable at all times"; validation rejects a UPI URL that is "incorrectly formatted, use[s] an unsupported protocol, refer[s] to a disallowed domain, or specif[ies] a non-permitted network port", and registration fails on "excessive URL redirects, redirects to a less secure protocol, or a response … that exceeds the system's handling capacity". File submissions are XML/JSON, max 100 registrations per file; "The DPP Registry does not store the full DPP data … The complete DPP data … remains hosted and maintained by you, the economic operator (or a service provider you designate). You are responsible for ensuring its availability and accuracy throughout the DPP's lifecycle". As of the guide, battery registration was not yet possible ("the semantic catalogue for this product group has not yet been defined").

### C. Harmonised standards — standardisation request M/604 and the EN 182xx series

18. **Standardisation request.** Commission Implementing Decision C(2024) 5423 of 31 Jul 2024 "on a standardisation request to [CEN, CENELEC and ETSI] as regards digital product passports in support of Union policy on ecodesign requirements for sustainable products and on batteries and waste batteries", amended by C(2025) 8024 of 28 Nov 2025 "as regards the legal basis and the deadlines" (both cited in Decision 2026/1736 recital 2; CEN-CENELEC refers to it as M/604). The Commission FAQ (12/2025, <https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/8748f8dc-3d48-4862-8153-e1165fb6be99_en>) lists the eight requested areas: unique identifiers; data carriers; "access rights management, information security, and data protection"; interoperability; "data processing, data exchange protocols, and data formats"; "data storage, archiving, and persistence to ensure long-term availability"; "mechanisms for data authentication, reliability, and integrity"; APIs for lifecycle management. Status: **in force** (request), deliverables see 19–20.

19. **Six harmonised standards cited — Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736 of 14 Jul 2026** (<http://data.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1736/oj>, in force on publication 15 Jul 2026). Annex: EN 18216:2026 *Data exchange protocols*; EN 18219:2026 *Unique identifiers*; EN 18220:2026 *Data carriers*; EN 18221:2026 *Data storage, archiving, and persistence*; EN 18222:2026 *APIs for product passport lifecycle management and searchability*; EN 18223:2026 *System interoperability*. Recital 5: they "satisfy the requirements set out in Articles 10 and 11". Status: **in force, presumption of conformity** under ESPR Art. 41(2). Normative text: paywalled (national standards bodies).

20. **Two security standards ratified, not yet cited.** CEN-CENELEC news 15 Jul 2026 (<https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2026/en-in-the-spotlight/2026-07-15-dpp/>) lists eight JTC 24 standards, adding **EN 18239** *Access rights management, information system security, and business confidentiality* and **EN 18246** *Data authentication, reliability and integrity*, "Six of these eight standards have been cited in the Official Journal". The Commission DPP page schedules an "Implementing Decision on the remaining two DPP standards" for September 2026; none found in the OJ as of 2026-09-03. Status: **adopted, not yet cited** — treat EN 18239/18246 as the imminent normative reference for restricted-data authorisation, ISMS expectations and data integrity, but without presumption of conformity yet.

### D. DPP service provider delegated act (ESPR Art. 11, third subparagraph)

21. **Status: under preparation, no draft text.** Have-your-say initiative 14382 / PLAN/2024/1820 (<https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say/initiatives/14382-Digital-product-passport-rules-for-service-providers_en>): call for evidence 12 Nov–10 Dec 2024 (178 responses); public consultation 8 Apr–1 Jul 2025 (275 responses); draft-act feedback and adoption stages not published. The call-for-evidence (Ares(2024)8031852) said "INDICATIVE TIMETABLE Q1-Q2 2026"; the Commission DPP page now shows "Delegated Act for DPP Service Providers" in Q2 and Q3 2027.

22. **Announced content (call for evidence + consultation questionnaire, EUSurvey 883326).** Requirements "preliminarily grouped in three areas: information security and (information) services of DPP data …; the financial viability of the DPP service providers to guarantee long-term access to DPP information; assurance for businesses that DPP service providers comply". Scope of activities: "(i) creating the DPP; (ii) creating the data carrier; (iii) registering the DPP; (iv) hosting (storing) the DPP; (v) query service …; (vi) enabling updates …; (vii) DPP reporting; and (viii) providing backup tools and services". Compliance options: "a self-declaration or (dedicated) enforcement efforts or an ex ante certification scheme". The questionnaire probes minimum availability tiers of 90/95/98/99 % "within 365 days", resilience measures (redundant servers, failover clustering, load balancers, auto-scaling, DDoS protection, monitoring and alerts), and names as data-security examples "securing the data integrity using a hashing mechanism like SHA-256, securing data authenticity using qualified digital signatures, or using encryption techniques when securing restricted data", with cybersecurity defined by reference to the Cybersecurity Act (EU) 2019/881. → Non-binding, but the clearest official indication of the future Control set; the register should pre-adopt them as anticipatory Controls flagged "source: draft policy".

### E. Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 — the first concrete DPP

Source: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng>. Status: **in force; battery passport applies from 18 Feb 2027** (Art. 77(1)); registry upload duty Art. 77(10) added by ESPR Art. 78.

23. **Access tiers — Art. 77(2) and Annex XIII.** Four classes: (1) public model-level data; (2) model-level data "accessible only to persons with a legitimate interest and the Commission" (detailed composition, part numbers, dismantling information, safety measures); (3) "accessible only to notified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the Commission" (test reports); (4) individual-battery data "accessible only to persons with a legitimate interest" (performance/durability values, state of health, status, usage-derived data). Art. 77(9): by 18 Aug 2026 the Commission adopts implementing acts on who has a legitimate interest and "to what extent they can download, share, publish and re-use that information" — see the battery-act status in F.

24. **Essential requirements — Art. 78** mirror ESPR Art. 11 and add the wording for authorised operators: (c) data "stored by the economic operator … or by operators authorised to act on their behalf"; (d) such operators "shall not be allowed to sell, re-use or process such data … beyond what is necessary"; (e) the passport "shall remain available after the economic operator … ceases to exist or ceases its activity in the Union"; (f) "the rights to access, introduce, modify or update information … shall be restricted based on the access rights"; (g) "data authentication, reliability and integrity shall be ensured"; (h) "a high level of security and privacy is ensured and fraud is avoided". Art. 77(3): QR code links to a unique identifier compliant with ISO/IEC 15459-1…6 "or their equivalent"; Art. 77(4): the operator "may give written authorisation to any other operator to act on its behalf"; Art. 77(8): the passport "shall cease to exist after the battery has been recycled".

## Implications for the Control Register

Concrete Controls the register can adopt now. "Source" cites the binding text; "Status" says whether the source is in force, imminent or anticipatory. Ring per map #772 (1 software · 2 reference deployment · 3 operated instances · 4 organisation).

## Open questions

1. **Normative clauses of EN 18216–18246 are unread.** Only titles/scopes are public; the register's Controls above cite standards by number, not clause. Purchase or library access is needed before #790 can map clause-level requirements (especially EN 18221 persistence/backup expectations and EN 18239 ISMS/access-rights model).
2. **Is open-dpp's SaaS a "DPP service provider" in every deployment?** Whitelabel instances run for one customer and self-hosted instances run by the operator are not independent third parties; the SaaS instance is. The register may need two Instance Profiles for the provider-specific Controls (DPP-PERS-02/03, DPP-REG-03, DPP-ACC-05).
3. **Who holds the legal back-up for open-dpp's own customers?** Art. 10(4) demands an independent provider; open-dpp cannot be both primary host and back-up provider for the same passport. #596 needs a partner/escrow design or a clear statement that its S3 copy is redundancy, not the Art. 10(4) back-up.
4. **Registry API contract.** IR 2026/1778 Art. 3(b) mandates an API; the August 2026 User Guide documents only UI and file upload (XML/JSON, ≤ 100 per file). API specification, authentication (EU Login OAuth? tokens?) and rate limits are not public yet — the DPP-REG Controls should be re-checked when the API documentation appears.
5. **Availability number.** The Commission consulted on 90–99 %; no legal figure exists. The register should pick a target (and evidence method) rather than wait.
6. **Battery-passport secondary acts.** The Art. 77(9) implementing act on legitimate interest was due by 18 Aug 2026; its adoption status and the "data point by category" guidance (15 Aug 2026) were not verified against the OJ in this pass.
7. **Reader credentials.** ESPR Art. 11 fourth subparagraph allows an implementing act on digital credentials of actors with access rights (e.g. EUDI-wallet attestations). Nothing adopted; the authorisation design in DPP-ACC-03 should not hard-wire a local account model as the only path.
